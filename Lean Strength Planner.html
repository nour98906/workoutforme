Weekly schedule

Weekly completion summary

Workouts

Each session is about 50–55 minutes including the warm-up. Choose your phase first: sets shrink in Week 1.

Rest times

Heavy compounds (bench, squat, leg press, Romanian deadlift): 2–3 minutes. Moderate compound and machine work: 90–120 seconds. Isolation and core: 60–90 seconds. Tap the timer on any card.

First 2 weeks: ramp-up

Rebuild your training tolerance gently so soreness stays low. Tap a card to switch the workouts to that phase.

Progression: double progression

Each exercise has a rep range. Stay at one weight until you reach the top of the range on every set with good technique, then add a small amount.

Example: 3 × 6–10

10 / 10 / 10 at about RPE 8

Every set hit the top of the range with controlled form. Add a small amount of weight next session (the smallest jump available).

10 / 9 / 8

Not there yet. Keep the same weight and try to add a rep or two next time.

Rules

  • Only add weight when technique stays controlled.
  • After adding weight, reps will drop. That is normal; build back up.
  • Do not train to failure on compound lifts. Stop with at least 2 good reps left.
  • No max-effort single-rep tests, ever.
  • A bad day? Repeat the weight or lower it. Consistency beats ego.

RPE guide

RPE (rate of perceived exertion) means how many good reps you still have left. This program mostly lives at RPE 7–8.

5-minute dynamic warm-up

Do this before every session.

0:00–1:00Easy treadmill, bike or rowing
1:00–2:00Arm circles + band pull-aparts
2:00–3:00Bodyweight squats + reverse lunges
3:00–4:00Hip hinges + walking lunges
4:00–5:00Workout-specific dynamic movements: shoulder rotations and scapular push-ups on upper days; leg swings and glute bridges on lower days

Then: light ramp-up sets

Before the first heavy compound exercise (bench, squat or leg press), do 2–3 lighter sets of that same lift, for example 8 reps at about half your working weight, then 5 reps at about 70%. Rest briefly between them, then start your working sets.

Nutrition

The goal is to support healthy growth, performance and recovery, with gradual body recomposition. This is not a cutting phase.

Protein: 1.4–1.6 g/kg/day

For 55 kg that is about 77–88 g per day. Spread it over 3–4 meals (roughly 20–30 g each) rather than one big serving.

Calories and supplements

  • No fixed calorie deficit should be prescribed to a teenager from height and weight alone. Do not diet aggressively.
  • Eat enough, regular meals with carbs, protein, fats, fruit and vegetables to fuel training and growth.
  • If you have concerns about weight or growth, talk with a parent/guardian and a pediatrician or sports dietitian.
  • Food first. No unnecessary supplements.

Recovery

😴

Sleep

Aim for 8–10 hours a night. This is when you grow and repair.

💧

Hydration

Drink water through the day and during training. Pale yellow urine is a good sign.

🛌

Rest days

Muscle is built during recovery. Do not add extra workouts on rest days.

🚶

Light activity

Walks, easy cycling or gentle stretching on rest days help you feel fresher.

🩹

Soreness and pain

Mild muscle soreness is normal and fades in 1–3 days. Sharp, pinching or joint pain is not: stop that exercise, and tell a parent or coach if it persists.

📅

Consistency

Four good sessions a week for months beats a few heroic weeks. Show up, move well, repeat.

Muscle frequency

Every major muscle group is trained about twice per week.

This planner is general education, not medical advice. Learn barbell lifts with a qualified coach or supervision, use safety equipment, and talk with a parent/guardian or doctor before starting if you have any health concerns.
